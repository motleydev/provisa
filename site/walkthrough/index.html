03 How to drive it

In the deck

A briefing is a set of tabs rather than a long page. One slide holds the screen; the next one wipes over it from the side you are travelling, and the slide's own rules, headings and figures draw as it lands. Nothing scrolls.

← → or space step a slide, Home and End jump to either end, and Esc leaves deck mode for the article. Clicking the outer third of either side steps too, and a swipe does on touch. The numbered strip across the top is the tab set: it marks where you are, names the slide you are on, and every number jumps.

Any slide is addressable — #s-07 is the seventh — so one slide can be sent on its own. Nothing advances by itself unless you switch on “advance automatically”, which is off every time the page loads, because a deck that starts moving on load takes the pace away from the reader.

Without the deck

Append ?view=page to any briefing, or follow the “Read as one page” link at the bottom left, and the same markup renders as one editorial article: every heading in order, every figure captioned, printable. That is also exactly what a browser with JavaScript disabled gets, because the deck is an upgrade of the article rather than a replacement for it.

If your system asks for reduced motion, nothing animates and every slide arrives in its finished state. No figure here is an image: the diagrams are drawn in CSS from the brand's own colours, so they invert with the theme and cost no download.
