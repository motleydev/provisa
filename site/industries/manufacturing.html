Plant floor and business systems. One governed model.

Plant-floor data and business systems in one model, so quality and supply questions are answered across both. A quality engineer sees a spike in returns and needs every unit built on the same line, shift and supplier lot, with the machine readings recorded while each was made. 01Scenario one — Quality trace

A quality engineer sees a spike in returns for one product and needs every unit built on the same line, shift and supplier lot, with the machine readings recorded while each was made.

Sensor readings stream in through the Kafka source and land as a live table. Batch and lot records come from the ERP database; returns come from Salesforce service cases. Relationships link unit to lot, line and shift, so the trace is one governed query. The Cypher surface walks the same model as a graph from the returned unit back to its suppliers. The engineer's agent returns that trace as a graph, drawn in the client with the same force, hierarchy or ELK layouts the explorer uses.

Related: Real-time operations, Materialized views

02Scenario two — Supplier scorecards

Procurement wants a weekly view of supplier risk: on-time delivery, defect rates and open quality cases per supplier, shared with each supplier so they see only their own scorecard.

A materialized view computes the scorecard from ERP receipts and quality cases on a schedule. A supplier portal role reads it over GraphQL with a row filter on supplier, so each supplier sees only its own rows. A data-quality checker flags late or missing receipt data before the scorecard is published, keeping each week's results.
